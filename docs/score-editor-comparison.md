# What Engraver should borrow from other score editors

Reviewed November 7, 2026. This is a comparison of documented workflows and firsthand user reports against the active Engraver workbench. It is not a timing benchmark or a survey of feature popularity. The order below reflects how often a feature would help in Engraver, how well it fits the local library and performance reader, and what must be built first.

**Start with a continuous note-entry mode.** Engraver is already useful for opening, correcting, and playing a score. Starting with a blank staff is slower: selecting a beat, choosing or typing a pitch, and moving to the next beat are separate actions. That friction multiplies with each phrase. The next editing release should let a musician enter eight measures, including chords and rests, without leaving the score.

## What the other apps do well

| Reference | Workflow worth learning from | What Engraver lacks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| MuseScore Studio | A visible note-entry cursor advances as you type pitches or rests. Durations have shortcuts. [Range copy](https://handbook.musescore.org/basics/copy-and-paste) preserves a phrase, including its rhythm, and can repeat it in place. [Note entry](https://handbook.musescore.org/basics/entering-notes-and-rests) supports keyboard, mouse, and MIDI. | The [score surface](../src/components/workbench/ScoreCanvas.tsx) edits notes and navigates existing beats; [Note details](../src/components/workbench/NoteInspector.tsx) can add to the active beat. There is no continuous pitched entry mode. [Copy selected notes](../src/store/editorStore.ts) puts them into a single destination beat; whole-measure copying is a separate tool. |
| Dorico | The [Jump Bar](https://www.steinberg.help/r/dorico-se/6.2/en/dorico/topics/user_interface/user_interface_jump_bar_r.html) finds commands and jumps to bars or rehearsal marks. [Text entry fields](https://www.steinberg.help/api/khub/documents/NNqtt0gVE9kWZRClp6krTQ/content) add markings at the caret. Its [Insert mode](https://www.steinberg.help/api/khub/documents/dU~Jkjs0u~2tXE3tplfzYg/content) makes the scope of a rhythmic insertion explicit. | Existing notation controls work, but finding a rare command takes browsing. There is no score-wide command search or rhythmic insertion that shifts following material in a selected voice. |
| Sibelius, Dorico, and MuseScore Studio | Sibelius [Dynamic Parts and Magnetic Layout](https://www.avid.com/sibelius) keep parts in sync and reduce manual collision cleanup; options vary by [edition](https://www.avid.com/sibelius/comparison). [Dorico layouts](https://www.steinberg.help/r/dorico-pro/6.2/en/dorico/topics/setup_mode/setup_mode_layouts_c.html) separate the musical content from how each part is printed. [MuseScore parts](https://handbook.musescore.org/basics/parts) can save their own layouts and choose instruments and voices from one score. | Engraver can display and print an individual instrument and adjust global spacing. It lacks named linked print layouts, independent part formatting, and batch PDF output. Imported layout details can reflow in the viewer. |
| Guitar Pro 8 | [Progressive speed](https://www.guitar-pro.com/docs/gp8/audio/playback/speed) turns a loop into an exercise. Its [chord diagrams](https://www.guitar-pro.com/docs/gp8/tools/chords-diagrams) help the player find alternative chord shapes. | Engraver has loops, speed, guitar techniques, and diagrams. It lacks automatic tempo steps and a tuning-aware browser that suggests playable shapes from a chord name. |
| Soundslice | Save named [clips](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/player/basic/302/clips/), align the score to a real recording with [sync points](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/creating/syncing/170/navigating/), and loop what you hear. [Focus mode](https://www.soundslice.com/help/en/player/basic/277/focus-mode/) lays out just the chosen bars, which helps on small screens. | Engraver has A–B focus and a local backing track, but the range is temporary and [audio alignment](../src/components/workbench/BackingTrack.tsx) uses one offset. A recording that speeds up or slows down can drift against the score. |
| forScore | Its [display controls](https://forscore.co/documentation/basics/) include partial page turns, and its [page links](https://forscore.co/documentation/tools/) can jump back for repeats. | Engraver already has setlists, page modes, bookmarks, annotations, and keyboard-style page turns. A per-piece option to reveal the next half page or jump between repeat points would address a different problem. |
| Flat, Noteflight Learn, and StaffPad | Flat [anchors feedback to notes](https://help.flat.io/en/education/music-notation-software/inline-comments/) and makes [history browsable](https://help.flat.io/en/music-notation-software/history/). [Noteflight Learn](https://support.noteflight.com/hc/en-us/articles/360020579032-Creating-Assignments-in-a-Standalone-Site) gives students copies to work on. [StaffPad](https://www.staffpad.net/) supports writing with an active pen. | Engraver has local snapshots and PDF annotations. It lacks score-anchored comments, one-piece sharing with selected attachments, and pen recognition. These solve different problems; there is no need to bundle them together. |

The most useful community signals were specific. [Former Finale users](https://forums.steinberg.net/t/gratitude-and-things-we-love-from-finale-refugees/956957) praised Dorico’s repeat-selection shortcut and automatic spacing. In a [Dorico favorites discussion](https://forums.steinberg.net/t/favourite-new-features/987250), musicians singled out keyboard access to properties, proofreading, and looping for transcription. A [discussion of Dorico popovers](https://forums.steinberg.net/t/shortcuts-and-longcuts/154087) praised typed commands while pointing out the burden of remembering separate shortcuts. A [TalkBass transcription account](https://www.talkbass.com/threads/transcriptions-with-soundslice-highly-recd.1681229/) describes lining up a recording with measures, slowing it to check timing, then practicing the resulting loop. [Avid’s 2022 mobile release](https://www.avid.com/resource-center/whats-new-in-sibelius-january-2022) identifies Dynamic Parts as the top request among its mobile users at the time. These accounts explain *why* the features are useful; they do not establish how many Engraver users would want them.

## Recommended order

1. **Make it possible to write a phrase without reaching for the inspector.** Add a visible **Enter notes** toggle and caret, one active duration, note or fret entry, rests, accidentals, a chord modifier, and automatic movement to the next rhythmic position. On a phone, reuse the same commands in a bottom strip with a compact piano or fret input. Give **New score** a small set of starting templates such as guitar, piano, and lead sheet; the current blank score starts with guitar. Start with step entry and predictable bar filling. Add MIDI keyboard input and voice-scoped insertion after the core timing rules are stable. Keep ordinary selection and playback shortcuts unchanged outside entry mode.
2. **Make passage edits safe and easy to find.** Copy a time range as beats and rests with their internal ties and markings. Start with one staff, make destination replacement visible, and make a paste or repeat one undoable edit. Add multiple staves, voice filters, and insert-paste after that works. Put existing commands behind one searchable **Find a command** button and a keyboard shortcut, with results like **Go to measure 24**, **Add a key signature**, or **Check score**. It should respect the current selection and show why a command is unavailable. Routine duration and pitch controls should remain visible. Extend the current score checker to flag other high-confidence problems only once the editor can offer a clear remedy.
3. **Give Perform a practice shelf.** Fix mixer changes that interrupt audio first. Then let a musician select a phrase, save it as **Bridge** or **Hard bars**, and start it again after reopening the piece. Add a simple speed trainer: for example, start at 65%, go up by 5 percentage points every three passes, and stop increasing at the chosen target. Put this behind the existing loop controls; show the saved passage name, tempo, and current repetition while playing. Anchor passages to the music so that inserting a measure does not silently move the loop to the wrong place. The bigger opportunity is to align a local recording to the score at multiple points, then let an original PDF, editable score, and recording belong to one piece.
4. **Make parts you can hand to players.** Keep one source of musical data and save a layout for each part. Start with part names, instrument selection, paper and staff size, and separate system breaks. Then add multimeasure rests, independent page breaks, concert/written pitch, and batch PDF export. Prove page and system control in the current renderer on a real quartet and a transposing ensemble before committing to advanced collision avoidance, cue extraction, or orchestral condensing.
5. **Add specialized tools when the workflow calls for them.** For guitarists, add a large, tuning-aware **Chord shapes** panel with preview and separate actions to insert a diagram or write notes. For teachers or arrangers, start with comments attached to a beat and an exportable single-piece bundle with selected recordings and PDFs. For live performers, test repeat links and half-page turns on a real tablet and pedal. Defer real-time cloud editing, handwriting recognition, large sound libraries, and full publishing controls until there is evidence of demand; they add substantial work beyond the editor and player.

An [audio export](https://handbook.musescore.org/file-management/file-export) is also useful for sending a rehearsal reference to someone who has no notation software. Engraver exports MIDI, which still needs a synthesizer to play. A render of the current mix is a better first audio investment than a large sample library, provided a prototype can produce reliable audio with repeats and tempo changes.

Judge releases on complete workflows. Enter an eight-bar melody with rests and a tied note on both a keyboard and a touch screen without opening a submenu for each note. Copy a mixed-rhythm phrase and verify that its durations, ties, and articulations survive one paste and one undo. For practice, reopen the same saved passage offline and change track volume mid-loop without restarting it. For parts, make one pitch correction in the full score and confirm that the regenerated part reflects it without losing its own layout.
